## The Code

```bash
# Set up credentials
cp .env.example .env
# Edit .env — add ALPACA_API_KEY and ALPACA_SECRET_KEY

# Pull options chains (current snapshot)
python ingestion/alpaca_options_backfill.py \
  --tickers SPY AAPL NVDA QQQ \
  --output-dir data/options/

# Inspect — how many contracts per ticker?
duckdb -c "
  SELECT symbol, option_type, COUNT(*) as contracts
  FROM read_parquet('data/options/**/*.parquet')
  GROUP BY symbol, option_type
  ORDER BY symbol, option_type
"

# What expirations does SPY have?
duckdb -c "
  SELECT DISTINCT expiration_date, COUNT(*) as strikes
  FROM read_parquet('data/options/**/*.parquet')
  WHERE symbol = 'SPY'
  GROUP BY expiration_date
  ORDER BY expiration_date
  LIMIT 20
"
```

## Key Concepts

**Options chain structure**
An options chain is the full set of listed contracts for an underlying security. For each expiration date, there's a series of strike prices. At each strike, you get a call (right to buy) and a put (right to sell). SPY has hundreds of expirations and thousands of strikes — a full chain snapshot is typically 10,000–50,000 rows.

**Bid / Ask spread**
The bid is what buyers will pay; the ask is what sellers want. The spread between them is the transaction cost for an options trade. A tight spread (e.g., $0.01 on SPY) means the market is liquid. A wide spread (e.g., $0.50 on a small-cap stock) means you'll lose money just entering and exiting. When screening for trades, filter for reasonable bid-ask spreads.

**Open interest**
Open interest is the number of outstanding (open) contracts — contracts that have been bought or sold but not yet closed, exercised, or expired. High open interest means the market is liquid and someone cares about that strike. Low open interest means thin markets and wide spreads. Screen for `open_interest > 500` before entering a trade.

**Implied volatility**
Implied volatility (IV) is the market's expectation of future volatility, backed out of the option's current price using Black-Scholes. It's expressed as an annualized percentage. If SPY IV is 20%, the market implies a ~1.25% daily move (20% / sqrt(252)). IV is the key metric for options traders — you buy options when IV is low, sell when IV is high.

## Common Gotchas

- **"401 Unauthorized"** — Double-check that your `.env` has the correct key and secret. Use paper trading credentials, not live.
- **"No data returned for ticker X"** — Alpaca's free tier covers US equities with listed options. Indices like `^VIX` won't work — use `VIX` or `VIXY` (the ETF).
- **"I only get today's data"** — Correct, that's the free tier. For historical chains, see EP 13 (Alpha Vantage).
- **"What does 'ask' of 0.01 mean?"** — For options priced under a dollar, the minimum tick is usually $0.01. A contract priced at $0.01 has almost no market. Avoid.
